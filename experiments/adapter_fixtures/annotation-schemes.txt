WHY MDD DEPENDS ON THE PARSER, NOT JUST THE TEXT
================================================================================
An explainer for the open question: should `parse_source` record the annotation
SCHEME as well as the FORMAT?

Everything below is computed by saphes, not asserted. Reproduce it with
    uv run python experiments/adapter_fixtures/scripts/annotation_schemes.py


THE SHORT VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mean dependency distance measures how far apart a word and its governor are.
That depends on WHICH WORD IS THE GOVERNOR — and that is a decision made by the
people who designed the treebank, not a fact about Hungarian.

Two linguists can annotate the same sentence, both correctly, by two accepted
conventions, and get MDD scores 22% apart. Nothing in the score says which
convention was used.

`parse_source` currently records "conllu" or "spacy". That is the FILE FORMAT.
It says nothing about the scheme, and the scheme is what moves the number.


EXAMPLE 1 — COORDINATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    "János és Mária tegnap moziba ment"
    (János and Mária went to the cinema yesterday)

The question: in "János és Mária", which of the three words is the head?

  Scheme A — Stanford / Universal Dependencies: the FIRST CONJUNCT heads.
  "és" and "Mária" both hang off "János"; "János" is the subject of "ment".

      1. János   -> ment     distance 5
      2. és      -> Mária    distance 1
      3. Mária   -> János    distance 2
      4. tegnap  -> ment     distance 2
      5. moziba  -> ment     distance 1
      6. ment    -> ROOT
                            MDD = 11/5 = 2.200

  Scheme B — Prague style: the CONJUNCTION heads.
  Both names hang off "és"; "és" is the subject of "ment".

      1. János   -> és       distance 1
      2. és      -> ment     distance 4
      3. Mária   -> és       distance 1
      4. tegnap  -> ment     distance 2
      5. moziba  -> ment     distance 1
      6. ment    -> ROOT
                            MDD = 9/5 = 1.800

  >>> 2.200 against 1.800. Same sentence, same formula, 22% apart.

Neither is wrong. Both conventions are used in published treebanks.


EXAMPLE 2 — POSTPOSITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    "a fiú az asztal alatt aludt"
    (the boy slept under the table)

The question: in "az asztal alatt", does the noun head the phrase, or "alatt"?

  Scheme A — UD: the NOUN heads, "alatt" is a `case` marker attached down to it.
      ... asztal -> aludt   distance 2
          alatt  -> asztal  distance 1
                            MDD = 9/5 = 1.800

  Scheme B — older style: the POSTPOSITION heads its own phrase.
      ... asztal -> alatt   distance 1
          alatt  -> aludt   distance 1
                            MDD = 8/5 = 1.600

  >>> 1.800 against 1.600, 12% apart.


WE ALREADY SAW THIS FOR REAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is not hypothetical. Over the same fifteen Hungarian sentences in
`corpus.txt`, HuSpaCy and emtsv disagree:

    engine     MDD      MHD    max tree depth
    HuSpaCy    1.6845   1.5492   4
    emtsv      1.5328   1.8128   6

They INVERT: HuSpaCy gives the higher MDD and the LOWER MHD. HuSpaCy builds
flatter trees with longer arcs; emtsv builds deeper trees with shorter ones.
That is a difference between the two annotation schemes, not between the texts —
the texts are identical.

A concrete instance of the same thing: emtsv attaches sentence-final punctuation
to 0, making it a second root. HuSpaCy attaches it to the main verb. So
`require_single_root=True` discards EVERY emtsv sentence and NO HuSpaCy
sentence, from the same fifteen inputs.


WHY THIS MATTERS FOR PUBLISHED NUMBERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If you write "Hungarian parliamentary speech has MDD 2.4", that number is only
comparable to another number produced under the same scheme. Compare across
schemes and you may be measuring the annotation convention rather than the
language.

Futrell, Mahowald & Gibson (2015) hit exactly this in their 37-language study
and dealt with it by NORMALISING the treebanks before measuring — they say so
explicitly in their Methods:

    "We normalize the corpora so that prepositional objects depend on their
     prepositions (where the original corpus has a case relation) and verbs
     depend on their complementizers (where the original corpus has a mark
     relation). For conjunctions, we use Stanford style."

That is precisely Example 1 and Example 2 above. They could normalise because
they held the corpora. saphes never sees the corpus — it is handed a parse and
measures it.


THE ACTUAL QUESTION FOR YOU
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
saphes cannot detect the scheme. It could, however, RECORD one, so that a result
carries the information a reader needs.

  Option 1 — do nothing.
      `parse_source` stays "conllu" / "spacy". The scheme is a methods-section
      problem for whoever publishes, not a library problem. Costs nothing.
      Risk: two of your own projects compute Hungarian MDD with different
      parsers and the results get put in one table.

  Option 2 — add a free-text `scheme=` parameter, recorded on the result.
      e.g. scheme="UD 2.x, Szeged Treebank conventions"
      Cheap, unvalidated, and only as good as what the caller types. But it
      makes the omission visible: a result with scheme=None is visibly
      unlabelled rather than invisibly ambiguous.

  Option 3 — Option 2, plus adapters filling in what they know.
      `from_spacy` could record the model name, `from_conllu` could read the
      `# newdoc` / treebank metadata that many CoNLL-U files carry.
      More work, still not verification, but no typing for the common case.

  Option 4 — normalise like Futrell.
      Convert incoming parses to one canonical scheme before measuring. This is
      the only option that makes numbers actually comparable, and it is a large
      piece of work: it means encoding the conversion rules for coordination,
      adpositions, copulas and complementisers, per source scheme. It is a
      project, not a parameter.

My recommendation is Option 3, but Option 1 is defensible if MDD is only ever
going to be computed inside one pipeline with one parser.
